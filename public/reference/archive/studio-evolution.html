Studio evolution

Dixon’s account connects changes in instruments, sequencing, mixing and monitoring with changes in the music he could make.

Artist

Hardware and musical space

Merritt Island

External samplers and synthesizers, a DOS sequencer and a compact console formed the early Cosmic studio.

Effects as arrangement

Dixon describes carefully programmed delays and selected reverbs as central to the spatial character of those records.

Larger consoles

After COSMIC 009, the Mackie 24-8 and expanded MIDI routing gave him more room to visualize and organize arrangements.

Digital instruments and recall

Time Travel

Logic sequencing and the Yamaha 03D changed the workflow. Digital mixing and mastering introduced new lessons in headroom, synchronization and loudness.

Aria — One

The 2001 remix represents tighter rhythmic alignment, expanded sampling and local effects processing in Dixon’s account.

Logic instruments

EXS24, Absynth, ES2, EVOC and later software tools expanded his cinematic palette and reduced the effort of recalling sessions.

Composition with greater control

Almost Flew

Dixon identifies this remix as a decisive application of scales to vocal placement and newly written melodies.

Pulse

A cinematic opening leads into a heavier dance-floor section, demonstrating the compositional control he wanted to express.

Preservation

Returning to archived hardware patches was rewarding, but also reminded him why recall and practical workflow mattered.
